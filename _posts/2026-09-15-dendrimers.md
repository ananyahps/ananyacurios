I spent two weeks of my summer banging by head against a table at least twice a day. In other words, I spent a lot of time writing chemistry code. Who knew that changing letters in strings could be so dang finnicky? I sure didn't.

Head-banging aside, my area of interest was actually REALLY cool. You see, there are these molecules called dendrimers, which are used in cancer treatment and drug delivery. They have three main parts: a core, a repeating dendron, and an end group.

Dendrons attach to the core and repeat onwards, creating "generations". Kind of the same concept as fractals. 

{% include split.html images="/assets/dendrimer.png" alt="a pic of a dendrimer" size="400px" caption="generation two of PAMAM" %}

They're SO cool. I found ways to construct these dendrimers quickly using the Wolfram Language at camp, and I had a lot of fun with it. You can read my funny journey with them [here](https://community.wolfram.com/t/wsrp26-recursively-constructing-and-analyzing-dendritic-polymers/27227). Spoiler: it did not go very well at all.
