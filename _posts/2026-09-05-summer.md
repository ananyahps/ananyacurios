I read a lot this summer, for the first time in what seems like a long time. 

Reading is, I think, one of the most fundamental parts of my existence, and has, though  unwittingly, been for a long, long time. My summer holidays in fourth grade consisted of four big collections of every Calvin and Hobbes strip written, and one very motivated tween. Obligatorily next came Harry Potter, then Percy Jackson, then a whole plethora of other quintessential books for the growing adolescent.
 s much as I would now like to say that I still consider reading incredibly important, and I read all sorts of books and so on so forth, that's just not true.

Hnestly,I never once thought I would be reading this summer. It didn't even occur to me as a possibility. According to my summer productivity schedule, I wouldn't be doing anything but work! Or so I thought.  Not only did I underestimate my own level of procrastination, I found myself in the midst of completely unpredictable circumstances where I had days on days to do literally nothing

I won't lie and say that I had a sudden epiphany to pick up a book and read, but my phone and laptop were dead, I happened to have a novel I'd been wanting to re-read for a while, and a lot of time on my hands. 

So, after five minutes of lying on the bed staring at the roof, I picked the thing up and finished it in one go.

The book was incredible, and I'm glad it was, else none of this reading adventure would have ever been started in the first place. The next day, by another pure stroke of luck, we went to the bookstore. 

Mind you, I love bookstores. Not just for the books, but for the atmosphere, new-book-smell, the nice people, everything. Not only that, we were in India, where the books were significantly cheaper than my usual residence in the United States. 

So I picked up an unhealthy amount of books, one of which included *The Inheritance Games* by Jennifer Lynn Barnes, and the rest is history. A history that included me finishing the entire nine book series, among many many many others.
