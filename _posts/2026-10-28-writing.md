title:  "writing - a poem"
permalink: /writing/

sometimes, i like to type without any choices, without the feeling of words on the page that don't look quite right, sentences that bend the wrong way and force shapes that don't feel right on my tongue as i read my words out loud. i don't have to worry about my spelling or the words i just typed, or any of those meddling things that stop the words from running out my mouth like a river. all i see are the words running from my brain to my hands to the page bleeding out of me like a cut that will never scab over. this is the feeling of writing that i love so much. the feeling of me and nothing else. my page is not constrained because my mind is not constrained by the rules of what my art should or shouldn't be. they call it stream-of-consciousness, but isn't that just another rule interrupting my narrative? a band-aid on my cut that needs to bleed, needs to bleed out all the wrongs and rights and rules that govern my existence, those metal bars that trap my art in a cage when it was meant to fly.

my writing is a bird and a river and a stream and the universe all at once, held back by nothing when i can't see what i've just written. my heart is swelling with emotion, but this is emotion none of you will ever see, none of you will ever read, none of you will ever feel, because i'm writing with invisible ink, writing for the sake of writing and not for anyone else's eyes, unless of course, i, by some miracle decide this stream of my consciousness is talented enough to share with you. there's another thought. what is talent really when it comes to art? is my writing only talented if it meets a criteria or fulfills a rubric? when i write on this page with invisible ink, does it show my talent? does it demonstrate that i am worthy of your praise, that i was born with some innate ability to create that holds me above you? what is the standard i am striving to meet and does it exists? if it exists, did i create it, or am i simply abiding by it? if it does not exist, why do i imagine it so? why do we, as humans, so often feel the need to create this imagined prison for ourselves and our reality? 

we are so drawn to rules and rubrics and criteria that we have forgotten what makes us so real: our art. we create like no other species creates. we write and we draw and we think and we sing, we pour our minds and our hearts into these beautiful shapes, perfectly cut each time because there truly is no standard of perfect. my words bleed from me like a cut and my heart cries out to you like a scream, not a scream of suffering, but a scream of something deeper. 

because i do think that our need to create, our unbridled desire to leave behind those little trails of ourselves on this shared world, on this shared existence, that drive to make art and sing and scream to the skies, it comes from somewhere deep, deep, inside us, buried away in the pockets of our minds the neuroscientists have never touched but the philosophers have. my art, written in invisible ink so that it may disappear, is here no just for you to see, but for me to feel. 
i am short-lived on this world, this world that has exploded and imploded and been a trillion different things all at once, compared to its billion years of wisdom, my fifteen years is nothing. a speck, a dot, a grain of sand in the much larger seafloor. this is where i am, and yet. and yet i create, because i feel as if my art is always worth creating. even if i am small, i create because i have to, because i was gifted a long time ago this beautiful thing called life and i intend to live it to the fullest. 

i am typing on this page in invisible ink not because i want it to disappear, but because i want to unclasp that pocket of me that makes things just to make them, that sings simply to sing, that bleeds just to feel the sting for a moment, then nothing. my words pour out of me like a raging river of thought, running though you and me, dear reader, this is my gift to you. for just a moment, you can watch my ink on the page and watch it disappear.

it's almost cathartic to watch my words and my art disappear just like i will one day. maybe it's some sick, ugly part of me that wants to see my words die because i will die, but is it so sick and ugly to want to make my art just like me? to make the one manifestation of myself on this universe fade away because i will fade away? and honestly, it truly is a pretty thing to have my art photographed and stored away in a memory. 

you might not remember me. you don't have to. my words will be like a photograph in the attic of your mind. perhaps one day, you will think about me. or maybe you won't. but the fact that you were here, whoever you are, that is enough. in all honesty, i'm not sure if you even exist. maybe i and only i will bear witness to these words and bear witness to my art. maybe i will never show this page with invisible ink on it in time for anyone to see the words. maybe one day i will think about this page, and i will smile, but i will not remember how i wrote, in length, about my joy. my blood, the life-breath that i give this page and my art, the creation i'm creating simply because i feel this desire to create something, these are things i will forget, but maybe it's also a pretty thing to forget. maybe it's a pretty thing for nobody at all to remember i was ever here because i remembered i was here. 

alas, all good things must come to an end, and this, my beautiful work, is most definitely a good thing. my pen is running dry, and the invisible ink is disappearing. i leave you here for now. good night.
